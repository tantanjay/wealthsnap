## 🧮 Calculation Accuracy Fixes
- **Debts Drag now matches your Runway**: Debts Drag shows how many months of runway your debt payments cost you. It compared two runways that both left out your savings goal contributions, so it measured against a runway you never actually see — and overstated the cost for anyone with an active goal (e.g. showing 1.0 months when the real figure was 0.7). It now counts goal contributions as part of your regular outflows, so the number lines up with the Runway on Home and Financial Health, and with the step-by-step math in Financial Health's explanation.
- **AI Runway matches your screens for new users**: When you had less than a full month of history, Home and Financial Health fell back to this month's spending to estimate your Runway, but the AI assistant had no fallback and left your everyday spending out entirely — so it could report a much longer Runway than the one you see. It now uses the same fallback, so Chat and your screens agree.
- **One Savings Rate everywhere**: The Savings Rate card on Insights treated debt principal you repaid as saved, while the Savings Rate Trend chart right below it treated it as spent — so the same month could read 80% on the card and 40% on the chart. Paying down a loan now counts as spent everywhere (the card, the chart, and your Monthly Summary), so the rate shows the share of your income you actually kept in cash, and the Help Center formulas match. Month-End Projection follows the same rule: its Projected Savings and Savings Rate subtract the principal you've repaid so far this month, shown as a line under Projected Savings. Before this month's payment lands the projection can read a little high; by month end it matches the card.
- **Goal-funded purchases no longer blow your budgets**: When you bought something from a savings goal — say a ₱50,000 trip from your Travel Fund — it still counted against your ₱5,000 Travel budget, showing the category as 1,000% over budget in Budget Health, the Home budget figure, and the spending breakdown. Smart Suggestions would also average that trip into a much higher monthly Travel budget. The goal was the budget for that purchase, so it no longer counts against category budgets or Smart Suggestions anywhere.
- **Monthly Summary investment amounts fixed**: Investments entered in a foreign currency were converted into your currency twice in the Monthly Summary and the data sent to Chat, so a $100 stock purchase could be reported as roughly 58 times its real peso value. Amounts are now converted once, matching what you see on the Investment screen.
- **Debt fees count as spending again**: Since 1.17.0 on Financial Health and Chat, and 1.18.0 everywhere else, fees you pay on a loan, like a processing fee or a monthly insurance charge, were left out of Burn Rate on the assumption that your minimum payment covers them. That change correctly stopped debt interest from being counted twice, but swept fees out with it. It doesn't; it only covers principal and interest. So those fees never counted anywhere, and Runway, Debts Drag, Debt vs Life, the Safe-to-Spend daily allowance, and the Runway Chat sees all looked a little better than reality. Fees now count as normal spending everywhere, while interest stays inside your minimum payment so it isn't counted twice.
- **Runway-drop alert uses your real balance**: If you log a bill ahead of time (say, rent dated the 30th), your Cash Balance counts it right away, but the runway-drop alert left it out and compared against a slightly different balance. Financial Health's Net Flow for this month left it out the same way. Both now use the same balance as every screen.
- **Runway alert and Runway Change match your screens**: The runway-drop alert, which warns you when your Runway falls 25% or more since last month, worked out Runway its own simpler way: it left out your minimum debt payments and savings goal contributions. So it could warn you about, or miss, a drop the Runway on Home never showed. Runway Change ("+0.4 months since last month") had the same problem on a smaller scale: Home and Financial Health each calculated it their own way, so the two could disagree if you'd logged something for a later month, or had only started using the app last month. All three now use one shared calculation, so the alert, Home, and Financial Health always agree.

## 🐛 Bug Fixes
- **Gemini API key field no longer hidden while typing**: On small phones, opening the keyboard in Gemini AI Settings squeezed the modal so much that its three buttons covered the API key field, so you couldn't see what you were pasting. Modals with text fields now get more room while the keyboard is open, and "How to get an API key?" moved from the footer to a link right under the field, leaving just Save and Cancel at the bottom.
- **Correct amounts for lower-value foreign currencies**: Investments entered in a foreign currency are stored in your own currency and converted back for display. That conversion was skipped whenever the asset's currency was worth less than yours (e.g. Japanese yen for a peso profile), so the investment options and history screens showed your peso amount with a ¥ sign. They now convert back correctly for any currency.
- **Savings Goals guide corrected**: "How Savings Goals affect your numbers" said a goal contribution counts toward Avg Daily Spending. It doesn't: Avg Daily Spending only averages your expenses, and a contribution is money moved into the goal, not spent. The guide now lists only Burn Rate, Runway, and Safe-to-Spend.

## 🎨 UI Polish
- **Disclaimers moved to the bottom**: On Debt Strategy and Financial Health, the disclaimer used to be the first thing you saw, pushing your actual numbers down the screen. It now sits at the end, after all the cards, so your debt-free date and runway are front and center. All disclaimers across the app now share one design, including the cost-estimate note in Gemini API Usage and the PIN setup warning, which keeps its warning color but matches the others' layout and icon.
- **Net Worth card says what it measures**: Home's Net Worth includes the interest you're projected to pay over the rest of each loan, not just what you owe today — which is why its Liabilities figure was higher than Total Debt on the Debts card right beside it. The card now reads "Projected Net Worth", with "Debt + Interest" instead of "Liabilities", matching the explanation you get when you tap its info icon.
- **See what you spent from your goals**: Buying something from a savings goal is real spending you'll want to see, but the money was set aside in earlier months. Different screens handled that differently: Insights' Top Category could show a ₱50,000 trip while Total Expense beside it showed only ₱10,000, and Home's Monthly Net subtracted the trip in full. Now every expense total shows your everyday spending with goal purchases on their own line underneath ("+ ₱50K from goals") — on Insights' Total Expense, History's Expenses, and Home's Monthly Net — and the spending breakdown marks how much of each category came from a goal.
